Website design and development

A business website should communicate clearly, work smoothly on mobile and make the next step obvious. We plan the structure around the audience and business goals, then bring together layout, typography, content, responsive behaviour and functionality. Depending on the project, this may include company profile websites, service websites, portfolio sites, landing pages, custom web applications or e-commerce stores.

E-commerce and online stores

An online store involves more than displaying product photographs. Customers need clear product information, simple navigation, useful filters where appropriate, a reliable cart and checkout journey, clear delivery information and order communication. We consider the product catalogue, customer journey and business operations when planning an e-commerce project.

SEO and digital marketing

A website needs to be discoverable by the people it is intended to serve. SEO work can include technical accessibility, useful page structure, search-focused content, internal linking and local business information. Digital marketing may also include campaign planning, landing pages, creative assets and measurement. We do not promise guaranteed rankings or instant sales; the goal is to build a clear strategy and improve it using evidence.

Brand identity and creative design

A consistent visual identity helps customers recognise a business across its website, social channels and promotional materials. Our wider creative work can include logo and brand identity, social media creatives, banners and brand-film concepts. These elements should support the same message rather than feel like disconnected pieces.

How we approach a project

The first step is understanding the business, audience and desired outcome. Next, we define the scope: pages, features, content, integrations, timeline and responsibilities. We then plan the structure and visual direction, develop the experience, review it across devices, test forms and key actions, and prepare it for launch. After launch, we can discuss maintenance, updates and future improvements based on the agreed scope.

The purpose of a portfolio is not just to display screenshots; it should help a prospective client understand the kind of problem a project addressed and the type of digital experience created. Explore the project pages on our website for examples.

What makes a good digital partner?

A good partner asks questions before quoting, explains what is included, communicates trade-offs and is transparent about costs and responsibilities. The business should retain clear ownership of its domain and important accounts. Timelines, revision rounds, content responsibilities, third-party fees and post-launch support should be discussed before work begins. We want clients to understand the process rather than feel dependent on unexplained technical decisions.
