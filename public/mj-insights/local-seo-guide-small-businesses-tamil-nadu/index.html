1. What local SEO actually means

Local SEO is the work of helping search engines understand what your business offers, where it operates and which nearby searches it may be relevant to. It can influence visibility in Google Search and Maps, but results vary by query, location, competition and the searcher's context.

2. Understand the three local ranking factors

Google explains local results mainly through relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well a profile matches the search. Distance is how far the business is from the searcher or location used in the query. Prominence relates to how well-known a business is, using information such as links and reviews. You cannot control distance or pay Google for a better local position.

3. Set up a complete Google Business Profile

Use the real-world business name and the most accurate primary category. Keep address or service area, phone, website, opening hours and special hours accurate. Add services, attributes and genuine photos where relevant. Verify the profile and keep information updated when the business changes.

4. Build useful location pages on your website

A location page should provide genuine local value: services offered in that area, how customers can contact you, service coverage, relevant project examples and practical details. Do not create near-identical pages for every town by swapping place names. Thin doorway-style pages do not help visitors.

5. Keep business details consistent

Use consistent business name, address and phone details on your website and reputable directories where the business is genuinely listed. Correct outdated phone numbers, duplicate profiles and old addresses. Consistency helps customers avoid confusion; it does not mean adding your business to irrelevant directories.

6. Earn genuine reviews and respond professionally

Invite real customers to share honest feedback without incentives or pressure. Never buy reviews, create fake accounts or ask only happy customers to review. Respond politely, protect private information and treat recurring criticism as operational feedback.

7. Create content that answers local questions

Publish useful service explanations, pricing factors, project case studies, FAQs and guides that address real customer questions. For a local service provider, a clear page explaining what is offered, where the service is available and how to request a quote is more valuable than repeating a town name unnaturally.

8. Measure local visibility and enquiries

Use Google Business Profile performance data where available, Search Console for organic search queries and landing pages, and analytics or enquiry tracking for calls and form submissions. Track meaningful outcomes such as qualified enquiries, not just impressions.

9. A 30-day starter plan

Week 1: verify profile details, categories, hours and website links. Week 2: improve the most important service page and contact journey. Week 3: request honest feedback from customers and publish one genuinely useful local guide or project story. Week 4: review Search Console and profile performance, fix errors and plan the next improvement.

Practical next stepUse the points above as a checklist. Prioritise the issues that affect your customers first, record what you changed and review the result rather than assuming every change will produce an immediate ranking or sales improvement.

Frequently asked questions

Does adding a city name everywhere improve local SEO?

No. Use location terms naturally where they help users understand your service area. Repetitive keyword stuffing makes content harder to read and does not create genuine local relevance.

Can a service-area business hide its address?

Google Business Profile has specific rules for service-area businesses. Follow the current profile guidelines and show an address only when customers can actually visit the staffed location during stated hours.

How long does local SEO take?

There is no fixed timeline or ranking guarantee. Profile verification, competition, website quality, reviews and local demand all affect progress. Measure trends over time rather than expecting an immediate position.
