1. What is Google Business Profile?

Google Business Profile is a free tool for eligible businesses to manage their business information on Google Search and Maps. It is not a replacement for your website: your website can explain services in depth, while the profile provides key details such as category, hours, location or service area, contact options, photos and reviews.

2. Check eligibility and represent the real business

Use the profile for a business that meets Google's eligibility rules and has in-person customer contact at a stated location or serves customers at their locations. Online-only businesses that do not meet the in-person contact requirements are not eligible. Do not create profiles for virtual offices or locations that do not genuinely represent the business.

3. Create or claim the profile

Sign in with a Google Account controlled by the business. Search for the business to see whether a profile already exists. Claim the existing profile where appropriate instead of creating a duplicate. Follow Google's prompts to add the real business name, category, location or service area, phone and website.

4. Choose an accurate business category

Select the category that most closely describes the primary business activity. Add relevant additional categories only when they accurately represent distinct services. Categories should describe the business, not list every keyword or product a customer might search for.

5. Set location, service area and hours correctly

If customers visit your staffed location, enter the real address and ensure the signage and customer access meet Google's guidelines. If the business travels to customers, configure service areas according to the rules and hide the address when required. Keep normal and special hours current.

6. Complete verification and protect access

Google determines which verification methods are available. Follow the method offered in the profile; do not assume a specific method will always be available. Keep ownership with a business-controlled account and add team members through appropriate access roles rather than sharing passwords.

7. Add services, photos and useful details

Describe actual services in plain language. Add genuine, recent photos that accurately represent the premises, team, products or completed work. Avoid misleading edits, stock images presented as real premises, promotional claims that cannot be substantiated or keyword-stuffed business names.

8. Manage reviews within policy

Ask customers for honest reviews based on genuine experiences. Do not offer discounts or gifts in exchange, suppress negative reviews, post reviews for customers or selectively solicit only positive feedback. Reply in a professional way and never reveal confidential customer details.

9. Understand local ranking

Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Complete information can improve relevance; distance depends on the search context; prominence may reflect how well-known a business is, including web links and reviews. There is no way to request or pay Google for a better local ranking.

10. Monthly profile maintenance checklist

Check contact details, hours, website link, category, service list, photos, review responses and duplicate or unauthorised profiles. Review performance data when available and compare it with website enquiries and real customer conversations.

Practical next stepUse the points above as a checklist. Prioritise the issues that affect your customers first, record what you changed and review the result rather than assuming every change will produce an immediate ranking or sales improvement.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Business Profile free?

The profile management tool is free. Be cautious of anyone claiming they can sell a guaranteed Google Maps ranking.

Can I have more than one profile?

Only when the business has genuinely eligible, distinct locations or business entities that meet Google's rules. Do not create duplicate profiles for the same location.

Can I add keywords to my business name?

Use the real-world business name as represented consistently on signage, stationery and customer-facing materials. Adding extra services or locations to manipulate ranking can violate guidelines.
